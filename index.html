    Overview

    People, land & community

    The Anishinabe called themselves “the people.” Their traditional territories included the wooded country of northern and central Ontario and southern Manitoba. Some communities later moved westward onto the Plains.

    A culture shaped by its environment.

    Thick forests, lakes, rivers, and wild rice all influenced the Anishinabe way of life. Hunting, gathering, community organization, and traditional teachings were closely connected to the land.

    World view

    The Seven Values

    Traditional teachings describe seven main values that guided how people should live and interact with Creation.

    Cycle of life

    Mamomin: Wild Rice

    Wild rice was an important food source for the Anishinabe and played a central role in their way of life.

    Living with the harvest

    The Anishinabe believed that if people were in harmony with nature, the rice crop would return. Harvesting was organized around protecting the resource.

      Government

      Leadership & balance

      The Anishinabe organized themselves into seven clans. Each clan had responsibilities that helped meet the needs of the community.

      Choosing leaders

      Clan leaders were chosen because they demonstrated qualities such as courage, good character, or skill in hunting.

      Solving disagreements

      The Crane and Loon clans had different leadership responsibilities. When conflicts occurred, the Fish clan helped settle disputes.

      Community

      The Role of Women

      The Anishinabe believed in equality and balance. Men and women were considered partners in the annual cycle of work, with different responsibilities that contributed to the community.

      Traditional teachings

      The Seven Grandfathers

      Traditional teachings explain that the Seven Grandfathers gave important gifts and lessons to the people. Edward Benton-Banai recorded one version of the teaching in The Mishomis Book. Select a card to read each teaching.

      A central idea
      Living in harmony with all of Creation was an important part of Anishinabe traditional teachings.

      The teachings, community systems, harvesting practices, and values described in this chapter all connect people to one another and to the natural world.
